Momo Break · Chrome extension · evals

I tested my own product against my own spec. It failed 10 of 49.

Momo Break nudges you to take a break. After about 50 minutes of work, a small peach mascot slides into the corner of your screen and offers you a ten-minute checklist: drink water, check your posture, look out the window. I built it, used it, and thought it worked.

Then I wrote down every rule I had promised and checked each one against the code.

What I was worried about

A break reminder has one job that matters, which is not being annoying. If it shows up during a screen share or at 6:06pm, people uninstall it. There's no AI in the trigger logic and I wanted to keep it that way, but that meant the only thing standing between me and a bad experience was my own testing, and I only ever tested the happy path.

So I turned the spec into 49 checks, ran them against the real extension code, and read 30 generated pop-ups myself to see what felt off. Ten checks failed on the first run, and six more were judgment calls I'd never actually made.

What broke

I grouped the ten failures by cause, and most of them weren't a wrong line of code. They were rules that collided with each other.

The worst one was the gentle pop-up, the softer version Momo sends after you snooze twice. It skipped every guardrail. I snoozed at 17:55 and it showed up at 18:06 anyway. The same shortcut also let it ignore the daily cap of six and the three-skip stop. One code path, three failures. The fix was three extra conditions on a single if.

Snooze was the other one. The spec said ten minutes and Momo took fifty. Showing a pop-up reset the work timer, so a snooze quietly meant starting a whole new cycle. You'd never find that by clicking around. You'd have to snooze and then wait fifty minutes, and nobody does that.

The checklist is supposed to open with a quick win, water or posture. In 14% of sessions it gave neither, because stretch and "look out the window" were also flagged as quick and could take the slot.

Two items in my spec turned out not to exist. Nothing in the code detects a screen share, and nothing waits for you to stop typing. Both still fail, and I've left them failing on purpose.

I'd rather have a promise I haven't built show up as a red line in a report than as a sentence in a README.

When the test was right and when I was

Not every failure meant the code was wrong. Sometimes my spec was. I wrote that "active" means input in the last two minutes, but the code uses sixty seconds, which is stricter and simpler, and nothing depended on the longer window. I changed the spec. Same for 18:00, which the code counts as inside work hours, and for "feeling better" over my original "feel better".

Other times I changed the code, when a product promise was at stake. With no web page open, the extension used to open a google.com tab just to have somewhere to draw. The tagline says Momo never blocks your screen, so I removed it. Now it sends a notification and waits.

Here is the pass count after each run as I worked through them. It isn't at 49 yet. What's left is either a feature I haven't built or a decision I haven't made.

Can these tests even fail?

A suite that always passes tells you nothing. So I broke the code on purpose nine different ways: cooldown from 15 to 5 minutes, max pop-ups from 6 to 7, an off-by-one on the skip limit, and a few more. The checks caught all nine.

Where this falls short

The harness fakes Chrome, so it proves my logic is right, not that Chrome behaves the same way. I run twelve manual checks in a real browser for the things a script can't see: fullscreen video, screen share, whether the pop-up pulls you out of another app. And passing every test only means Momo follows my rules. Whether anyone likes it needs real skip and completion data, which I don't have yet.

If I add AI

Today, six hand-written greetings beat a model on cost, speed and predictability, so I haven't added one. If that changes, these checks are the bar it has to clear, along with a judge I've validated against my own labels. One check already shows 16% of pop-ups repeat the previous greeting. That's the first thing I'd fix, with or without a model.

To run the checks on your own copy, from the extension repo:

cd momo-evals
node run-evals.js ..
